## 2.2 Unique mechanism: the warmth anchor sequence

Every competitor sells the same thing under different names: be more confident, ask for more, know your worth. The research says women already ask, at a slightly higher rate than men, and get charged for it. So confidence training sells a cure for a disease the buyer does not have, which is why the category has terrible outcomes and good refund rates.

The mechanism here starts from the opposite premise.

> The ask is not the problem. The unaccompanied ask is the problem.

An assertive request, delivered alone, triggers a competence-versus-likeability trade that costs a woman the promotion even when she wins the number. The boardroom research on how senior women actually manage this found two tactic classes working together: competence tactics to establish the claim, warmth tactics to keep the room. Neither one works by itself. Competence alone reads as aggressive. Warmth alone reads as unserious.

The Warmth Anchor Sequence is those two, in a fixed order, with scripts:

| Step | Name | What she does | What it neutralises |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The third-party anchor | The number enters the room attached to market data or a written band, not to her wanting it | Removes the "she is asking for herself" read that triggers the penalty |
| 2 | The scope ledger | A one-page written record of scope taken on since the last comp change, in the employer's own units | Converts a feelings conversation into an accounting one |
| 3 | The warmth frame | A fixed sentence pattern that attaches the ask to a shared outcome before the number lands | Keeps the likeability score intact while the competence claim is made |
| 4 | The silence hold | She stops talking. Scripted, timed, rehearsed | The single most common failure point. Most women talk the number back down themselves |
| 5 | The written follow | Same-day written summary that locks what was agreed in the manager's own words | Prevents the quiet walk-back, which is where most verbal wins die |

It is believable because it explains why her previous attempts failed without blaming her, and because each step maps to a documented failure mode rather than a motivational idea.

## 2.4 Time to first value: the Ask Tax Audit

Delivered instantly at checkout, completed in under 20 minutes, returns one number.

She enters her title, level, market, tenure, last comp change and scope added since. It returns:

> **Your ask tax is $27,400 per year.**
> Here is the breakdown. Here is what it compounds to by 2031.

That number is the product's entire first impression. It is concrete, personal, uncomfortable and impossible to un-see. It converts a vague sense of being underpaid into a figure she will repeat out loud to her partner that evening, which is also the referral engine.

Build the calculator off published pay-transparency band data by title, metro and level, not off self-reported survey averages. The defensibility of that number is the defensibility of the product.

## 2.6 Obligation classification

**COMPETITIVELY_REQUIRED**, honestly scored. Nothing forces this purchase.

That is the structural weakness of the offer and the reason the urgency has to come from her calendar rather than from the category. Everything in file 01 section 1.4 exists to compensate for this line. Do not let the copy pretend the obligation is higher than it is; buyers in this ICP are analytical and overclaiming reads as a scam.

The B2B route in file 00 does carry real obligation pressure, through EEO-1 reporting, federal contractor requirements and attrition costs a VP has to explain. That is a later build.
