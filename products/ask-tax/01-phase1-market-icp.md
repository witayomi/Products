# Phase 1: market screening, ICP and verbatim

Agent 1 output. Run against the brief as given, then against the narrowed market.

## 1.1 Non-industry filter

| Candidate market | Real industry? | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| "Women in male-dominated industries" | No | A demographic across dozens of unrelated fulfilment structures. A diesel tech, a surgical resident and a VP of platform engineering share no vocabulary, no pay mechanics, no buying trigger and no price ceiling. Nothing clones. Disqualified. |
| Women at senior-IC to first-line-leader level in commercial construction, engineering and industrial or enterprise tech, $110k+ | Yes | Homogeneous comp mechanics (bands, levels, calibration cycles, promo packets), a shared calendar (annual or semi-annual review), a shared vocabulary, and a defined income floor. One sales page, one script pack and one ad account serve all of it. |

The narrowing is what makes the offer scalable. It is also what makes it expensive, because you can price to a $140k earner in a way you cannot price to an undefined consumer.

## 1.3 ICP definition

| Attribute | Definition |
|---|---|
| Job titles | Project manager, project engineer, superintendent, estimator, design engineer, engineering manager, senior or staff engineer, product manager, operations manager |
| Verticals | Commercial construction and trades management, civil and mechanical engineering, industrial manufacturing, enterprise and infrastructure tech |
| Income | $110,000 to $260,000 |
| Age | 29 to 45 |
| Tenure | 4 to 15 years in field, typically one or two promotions in, stalled before the next |
| Team context | She is one of one, or one of two, women at her level on her site, floor or org chart |
| Buying moment | A written offer in hand, a comp cycle inside 90 days, a promo packet due, a new manager, a peer promoted past her |
| Explicitly excluded | Students, career changers with no current role, sub-$70k earners, anyone shopping for validation rather than a number. These are the refund cohort |

**Upmarket bias applied.** Target the senior end of every band. A staff engineer and a junior engineer take identical fulfilment effort and differ by roughly 3x in what they will pay.

## 1.4 The urgency fix

Category urgency is a 3 and cannot be improved. Buyer urgency can be manufactured, because it already exists on her calendar.

Do not target a demographic. Target a date.

| Trigger | Ad targeting or creative cue | Why it converts |
|---|---|---|
| Offer in hand | "You have the offer open in another tab" | 72-hour decision window, dollar value already known to her |
| Comp cycle inside 90 days | Seasonal flighting against Q4 and Q1 review calendars | Fixed deadline, single annual shot |
| Promo packet due | "Your packet is due in three weeks and you are writing it like a status report" | Deadline plus a document she is actively struggling with |
| Peer promoted past her | "He got the title. You got the workload" | Acute, specific, dated |

This is the highest-leverage decision in the launch. Demographic targeting on this offer produces a 1.3x ratio. Moment targeting is what moves it past 2x.

## 1.5 Verbatim bank

**Status: 7 of 20 minimum sourced. This is the one open blocker before launch.**

I am not inventing customer quotes to fill a quota. What follows is sourced. The gap and the plan to close it are below.

### Primary pain points

1. "People are still surprised to find a female superintendent, or women in the construction industry in general." (Construction Dive interview, female superintendent, Adolfson & Peterson)
2. Women in construction "are vastly outnumbered and have to prove themselves so much more than men." (Fieldwire testimonial series)
3. Women in engineering describe needing "to prove their credibility while being mindful of how they present themselves," because identical behaviour reads differently from a woman than a man. (Galloway engineering, International Women in Engineering Day)

### Negative circumstances

4. A product-marketing manager at Microsoft raised a solution in a partner-channel meeting and was ignored. Her manager later answered the same question with "What Jill said." (NBC News)
5. A journalist who raised that she was not being credited for her work was told by her boss that she was "being too emotional." (Womaning)
6. An automotive business owner: "A lot of people made fun of me and tried to sabotage my business, or take credit for the successes I have had." (Fortune)

### Desired outcomes

7. A female superintendent's summary of what changed things for her: "be assertive." Which is exactly the advice the backlash research says gets penalised, and is why the category keeps failing. (Construction Dive)

### The gap and how to close it

13 more statements needed, weighted toward desired outcomes, which is the thinnest section and the one that writes the sales page.

| Source | Method | Target yield |
|---|---|---|
| r/civilengineering, r/construction, r/ExperiencedDevs, r/womenEngineers | Search "promotion", "comp", "passed over", "counter offer". Pull only posts from people who state a title and a level | 8 |
| Blind (teamblind.com) threads under Compensation and Women in Tech | Same filter. Blind skews senior and states levels, which is exactly the ICP | 5 |
| Post-purchase survey, first 25 buyers | One open question: "what was happening in your job the week you bought this?" | ongoing |

**Rule for all of it:** capture the exact phrasing, including the job vocabulary. "Calibration", "comp band", "promo packet", "scope", "change order", "sign-off", "billable". Those words are the ad copy. "Empowerment" is not, and never appears in anything the buyer actually wrote.
